Why UAE fabrication shops overpay for welding consumables — and how to fix it

New Asian General Trading LLC · August 2026 · 5 min read
← Back to Insights

A workshop manager at a steel fabrication company in Al Quoz was spending AED 4,200 a month on grinding discs. His supplier had been on the approved vendor list for four years. Nobody had requested a competing quote in that time. When a second supplier was finally invited to quote the same specification, the price came in at AED 2,800 for identical product from the same manufacturer.

The difference was not product quality. It was four years of unchallenged pricing.

This is not unusual in UAE fabrication. Welding consumables — gouging rods, grinding discs, cutting wheels, welding wire, flap discs — are bought frequently, in volume, and often from a single supplier. That combination is exactly where overpayment hides.

The four ways fabrication shops overpay without realising it

1. Single-supplier inertia. Once a supplier is approved and delivering reliably, procurement teams stop looking. Pricing drifts upward gradually — a few percent per year — in a way that never triggers a formal review but compounds significantly over time.

2. Brand premium on standard consumables. For general structural fabrication, cutting, and surface preparation, the performance difference between a premium brand and a quality equivalent is often negligible. Workshops that specify premium brands across every category are paying for brand consistency rather than performance.

3. Urgency pricing. When a workshop runs out of stock mid-job, the supplier knows it. Urgent orders placed without alternatives attract pricing that bears no relationship to normal market rates. The root cause is inadequate stock management, not supplier opportunism.

4. Mismatched quantity breaks. A workshop buying grinding discs by the box when their monthly consumption justifies carton pricing is paying 15 to 25 percent more per disc than a buyer at the next tier up.

Why welding consumables are harder to price-check than most MRO categories

The same product — a 115mm cutting disc, a 6.3mm gouging rod, a 1.2mm MIG wire spool — can be sold under different product codes by different suppliers, making direct comparison difficult without knowing the underlying specification.

The practical test is this: ask your current supplier for their price on a specific item with a specific specification — brand, diameter, thickness, grade. Then ask a second supplier for the same specification. If the second supplier cannot match the specification exactly, that is useful information in itself.

What local UAE stock actually means for your costs

In the UAE welding consumables market, “local supplier” covers a wide range of actual supply chain positions. Some suppliers hold significant stock in UAE warehouses. Others take your order, source from a regional distributor, and deliver in 5 to 10 days while describing themselves as local.

When evaluating a new welding consumables supplier, the most useful questions are: what do you hold in UAE stock right now, where is your warehouse, and what is your standard lead time for an order placed today?

How to benchmark your current consumable spend in one week

  • Pull your top ten consumable lines by monthly spend — not by quantity, by AED value.
  • Record the exact specification for each item — brand, grade, diameter, thickness, product code.
  • Request a competing quote for each item. One additional supplier per item is enough for a first benchmark.
  • Check your quantity tiers. Ask your current supplier what their price break structure is for your top five items.
  • Check your buffer stock position. If less than five working days for any critical item, you are exposed to urgency pricing.

A practical checklist for reducing welding consumable costs

  • When did you last request a competing quote for your top five consumable lines?
  • Do you know your current supplier’s quantity break structure for your highest-volume items?
  • Are you holding adequate buffer stock to avoid urgency orders?
  • Have you evaluated quality-equivalent options for any OEM-branded consumables you buy regularly?
  • Do you know whether your current supplier holds UAE stock or orders on demand?
